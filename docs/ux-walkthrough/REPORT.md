# Recall Runner: first-time judge UX walkthrough

Reviewer: UX review pass, read-only. Date: 2026-10-25. Server: http://localhost:3217 (Next dev). No data was changed; I did not click "Start fresh demo" or "Approve campaign", did not sign in to PayPal, and did not open PayPal's pages.

Screenshot method: desktop shots are full-page Chrome captures at 1280 px (files `0N-*-desktop.png`). Phone shots are Chrome captures with 375 px device emulation (`m0N-*-375.png`). Sticky bars and the Next.js dev badge (round "N" at bottom-left) appear mid-page in full-page captures; that is a capture artefact and a dev-only element, not a finding. Findings from the interactive checks (keyboard, outbox tab, DOM measurements) have no saved image; the measured values are quoted in the table.

## (a) Summary of the judge experience

1. The home page explains the idea well in about 10 seconds (headline, 4 numbered steps, one big "Start fresh demo" button) and the buyer pages look polished and consistent.
2. The weakest point is the hand-off between the two roles: on the dashboard the judge must find the one buyer who can sign in among six, and the sandbox login is only "in the README", which a judge may never open.
3. Any other "Open buyer page" link (Outbox rows, the one-recall link) leads to a page that looks identical and offers "Confirm with PayPal", but the buyer is fictional; nothing warns the judge, so wrong clicks end in a confusing error.
4. At 375 px the dashboard is broken: the page scrolls sideways (scrollWidth 683 vs 375) and the case table hides Remedy, Amount and Status.
5. State words (Notified / Waiting / Settled) are not explained where they are used, there is no loading or cold-start page, and the buyer and error pages have no way back to the dashboard.

## (c) What already works well

- The home page tells a first-time visitor what the app is, who it is for and what to press, with a clear primary button and the time cost ("About 20 seconds").
- Role labels (MODEL / RULE / YOU / TOOL) and "Chosen by fixed rules, not AI" make it honest what the AI does versus the money rules; the wrong-account and old-link pages say "nothing was charged and nothing was sent".
- Status chips pair an icon with text (Notified, Waiting, Settled); colour is never the only signal. Focus rings are visible and consistent.
- The buyer page is clean: one clear action ("Confirm with PayPal"), a total ("$68.00 back"), and the reason for the sign-in ("so only you can claim this").
- The Test results page is a strong proof for the Technological Implementation criterion (51/51, the 180-day timeline).

## (d) Could not verify

- The "Start fresh demo" wait (about 20 s) and any progress text on it.
- The receipt screen after "Confirm with PayPal", the Settled state on the dashboard, and the time it takes for the webhook to arrive.
- Where "Confirm with PayPal" goes and what PayPal's sign-in page looks like (I did not click it, so this was not tested; only the link target `/api/auth/paypal/login?...` was read).
- A real cold start on Render (the server tested was a local dev server, so there was no 30 s blank page).
- Screen-reader behaviour; only Tab order and focus visibility were checked, and not on the buyer page in a live browser (the buyer page was checked by markup only).
- Console errors: only dev messages appeared (HMR, Fast Refresh, an unused-CSS preload warning for the buyer page); production behaviour is unknown.
- Dashboard "Why this happened" panel for rows other than Priya Nair and the full "How the AI found this recall" contents (9 steps listed, not expanded).
